About

I am an MS by Research student in Computer Science and Engineering at IIT Bombay, advised by the late Dr. Pushpak Bhattacharyya, Dr. Swaprava Nath (IIT Bombay) and Dr. Aditya Joshi (UNSW Sydney, Australia).

My research uses mechanistic interpretability to understand how language models represent meaning, and how that understanding can be used to guide them. One question I care about is form invariance: when the same concept appears in different forms, such as arithmetic written as symbols, as words, or as code, does a model process it with shared internal mechanisms, or with separate ones for each form? The other is steering: using what we learn about a model’s internals to shape its output at inference time. Figurative language is my main test bed here. Humor, sarcasm and humblebragging mean something different from what they literally say, which makes them a demanding target for both analysis and control.

I have also worked on multimodal retrieval augmented generation for document understanding, focusing on documents with complex layouts.

Before IIT Bombay, I was a Software Engineer at Cisco, building backend systems in Go. Outside research, I’m a longtime One Piece fan, patiently waiting for Oda to finish the manga.
